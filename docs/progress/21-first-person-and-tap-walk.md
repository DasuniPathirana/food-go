# First-person exploration and tap-to-walk

17 November 2026. This release adds the client's first-person request alongside the existing third-person view, improves the original avatars and reduces map-control clutter. Earlier age and email corrections remain in the same develop-target PR.

The owner explicitly limited this pass to the map. Feed is restored to its original first navigation slot, Quests second, Explore third, Deals fourth and Outback fifth. Passport remains the account/profile destination. The earlier age rule protects Feed content at its destination; it does not change those navigation labels. No other page is redesigned in this pass.

## Experience

First-person is the default for a new browser. The camera uses MapLibre's camera-from-location/altitude calculation, at 1.7 metres above the scene or terrain and aligned with the explorer's heading. It has a stable horizon and no head-bob. The character marker is hidden because the view is from that character's position. Third-person restores the overview and visible avatar. The selected view is a local cosmetic preference, independent of account permissions or XP.

The initial manual position lies on the actual Swanston Street vector, facing along its segment. A blue daylight sky uses the installed renderer's [documented sky configuration](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/sky/). Window resizing and full-map expansion recalculate the physical eye position while following; they do not recenter a camera the user has detached to browse.

A tap on a street starts a pedestrian-route request and then a virtual walk automatically. The Place explorer and Walk here buttons are removed. New taps replace older destinations; drag, pinch, zoom and tilt browse the map. Pause, Resume and End route remain deliberate controls. Provider failures leave selection and external Google/Apple directions available. A distant search chooses a new manual area; in live-location mode it browses the distant place without relocating the real GPS position.

Live GPS only plans a route after an actual fix has arrived. The avatar then follows the real device, not the virtual walk. Permission waiting, denial, hidden-page shutdown and manual browsing remain explicit. Neither camera movement, virtual walking nor avatar previews award verified XP.

Four original 3D characters now use more athletic proportions, connected articulated limbs, improved faces and hairstyles, layered expedition outfits, footwear and backpacks. Their grounded distance-based gait and gestures are preserved and refined. Walking and front/back previews help users choose before applying. Existing five IDs, saved choices and the original illustrated explorer remain compatible. Assets are generated by local geometry; no purchased asset or additional provider key is required.

Map controls have smaller icons and visible surfaces while preserving usable hit areas. The redundant follow button is consolidated into recenter. First/third-person choices sit above the map; location and avatar controls stay at its edge. Full-map expansion preserves the same renderer and state. Unsupported graphics use the labelled 2D map, with first-person disabled instead of a simulated camera claim.

## Control and motion contract

| Trigger | Result | Alternative, reduced motion and fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tap a street or press Enter at map centre | Request a real pedestrian path and start a manual virtual walk | Reduced motion or explicit pause prepares a paused route; Resume is deliberate. 2D supports the same route interaction. |
| Drag, pinch, zoom, rotate, tilt or keyboard pan | Stop automatic camera following and browse | Recenter restores following. Normal document scrolling remains available. |
| Select 1st or 3rd | Refocus at the explorer with the selected camera | No head-bob or cinematic travel. 2D explicitly provides third-person only. |
| Search a distant place | Rebase manual exploration or detach the live camera to browse | Never changes a raw GPS fix or awards a physical visit. |
| Enable live location | Ask for browser permission and follow actual fixes | No planning from a stale manual point while permission is pending. Denial preserves manual exploration. |
| Pause, hide page or enable reduced motion | Stop the relevant animation or virtual walk | Resume remains user-controlled; hidden scenes release animation work. |
| Avatar preview or action | Inspect original styles, walking, front/back and gestures | Static acknowledgement under reduced motion; illustrated fallback without WebGL. |

No page-scroll animation controls the camera. Touch viewport checks and browser tests are recorded below after the integrated confirmation; they do not establish performance on an untested physical phone or promise third-party availability.

## Verification

- Workspace type checks and **181 application tests** passed, including four focused camera cases. All **eight security convention checks** passed.
- The production build passed, followed by **167 local HTTP assertions**, including both camera choices and absence of the removed placement/walk buttons.
- Actual Chrome, at 1440 × 1000 and touch 390 × 844: the first-person eye was measured at 1.7 metres and at the explorer's geographic position. Third-person restored the 44-degree overview and visible marker; switching back restored the eye. Panning detached following, recenter restored it, and distant coordinate search rebased the manual eye. Both layouts retained Feed first, had no horizontal overflow and reported no runtime errors. Icons are smaller while control hit areas remain at least 40 pixels.
- Independent real-camera resize measurements confirmed **1.700 metres** through full-map open/close and viewport resizing. A physically dragged, detached camera retained its own centre after resizing.
- An independent **19-check Chrome routing audit** covered physical taps, rapid response replacement, cancellation, drag/pinch separation, explicit pause, reduced motion, GPS waiting/actual-fix planning, distant search and provider errors.
- The original avatar components passed **25 Chrome checks** and six focused motion/preference tests. Preview evidence uses a labelled local component fixture, without an account or a fabricated live reward.
