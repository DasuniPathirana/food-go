# Food Go

Food Go is an Australian food discovery and social product with a public catalogue, restaurant partner journey, interactive food exploration and a community of food stories. Web uses Next.js; native uses Expo and React Native; Supabase supplies the shared backend.

The visitor's main Explore task is to discover places through a highly interactive illustrated neighbourhood. The user specifically wants a Pokémon Go inspired sense of exploration with Food Go's own characters and artwork. Keep pan, zoom, walking, markers and ambient animation. The latest map correction requires the whole scene to fit beneath the header and above phone navigation, with a compact arrow pad on the map instead of a separate walking section. Secondary recommendations and avatar/shop details remain optional. Multiple shops in one mall should share one clear building marker and disclose the individual shops and floors.

Latest visual refinement, 14 November 2026: use a LIGHT theme with stronger food colours and familiar readable text, welcoming to all ages. Functional headings and body copy now use Arial/Helvetica with near-black ink `#111111` and secondary text `#414141`. Bricolage Grotesque remains only in the original Food Go wordmark. The neighbourhood-guide palette retains fresh white surfaces, tomato actions and butter-yellow XP. Desktop has a compact horizontal masthead so the approved map has more room. Feed shows stories sooner, trails pair food photography with a route, and Passport combines its compact identity cover with the owner's wall. Earlier expressive-heading fonts and dark or pale sage/lime directions are historical. Retain the map's approved bright dimensional character and interaction. Explore stays the first screen and centre navigation item; Feed is prominent. See DESIGN.md and the current delivery record for the implemented direction and its limits.

The owner clarified that other pages should have one natural document scroll with compact sections. Keep the first screen useful and retain longer content below it; do not freeze or hide an entire Feed, collection or Passport inside a single viewport. Passport's personal wall should sit below identity, in the familiar profile-and-timeline pattern, with posting and existing social actions. Extra game/profile detail expands on request so the wall is not buried beneath every milestone and activity entry.

The community experience has a public wall and personal walls where people share thoughts and food experiences, then like, comment and share posts. Food Go should carry familiar social interactions in its own visual identity. Public content needs moderation and reporting; users need blocking and control over their own posts. Clearly label visibility, moderation state and fictional previews. Personal media upload, following, messaging and notifications are separate features unless explicitly delivered.

Published venues may offer deliberate links to Google Maps and Apple Maps for real navigation. Every supported demo place may open its real suburb/area in those services, with explicit area labels; the three fictional Garden Arcade shops open Melbourne. Never navigate to fictional preview addresses. The illustrated scene is not a geographic map or proof of a visit. No simulated check-in, identity, ownership or reward may be presented as verified.

The standalone prototype remains an unchanged reference. The connected public catalogue currently contains no approved venue records. Preview venues, including the mall directory, are fictional and isolated from Supabase. Account and messaging flags remain closed; Brevo configuration is deferred; Mobile Message is intended for SMS sign-in codes. Do not put provider credentials in source, logs or chat.

The /demo namespace now joins example routes, arrival, simulated QR visits, separate dish confirmations, food trails, one-time rewards, XP, levels, badges, collections and social actions in one persistent browser store. Connected social XP and unique verified signed-in reach use a separate server ledger. Neither implementation turns local simulations into verified visits or redeemable rewards. Native map parity, real geographic positions, verified visit/dish rewards and complete partner/operator workflows remain future implementation. See the current progress records for verified delivery scope.

Optional avatar personalisation preserves the original illustrated explorer by default. Users may choose two rounded 3D or two anime inspired 3D alternatives, preview wave/celebration/look actions, and apply a browser cosmetic preference. Keep these controls in optional map/Passport panels, preserve map gestures and honour pause/reduced-motion preferences. Avatar actions cannot award XP or verify a visit. The 3D renderer is lazy loaded; static portraits and illustrated fallbacks remain usable without WebGL.
