# Food Go

Food Go is an Australian food discovery and social product with a public catalogue, restaurant partner journey, interactive food exploration and a community of food stories. Web uses Next.js; native uses Expo and React Native; Supabase supplies the shared backend.

Explore opens on a worldwide geographic overview with available 3D building data. Its compact camera control offers overhead, third-person follow, close third person and eye-level views, with the cosmetic choice saved in the browser. Layer settings open on request; a low-detail option reduces graphics work. Tap a street to start a pedestrian route and manual virtual walk. Tap the small explorer portrait to request live location and resume following, or hold it to customise the character below the scene. Keyboard users can locate with Enter and customise with Shift and Enter. GPS uncertainty, stale fixes and implausible jumps hold the cosmetic explorer in place; the accuracy circle still describes the raw device fix. Visitors can search a real place or type coordinates, select published restaurants, and open Google Maps or Apple Maps directions. World satellite imagery requires a configured licensed provider. The bundled Melbourne aerial and satellite overlays have explicitly local coverage. The map fills the available browser section and expands without replacing its canvas. These browser camera and procedural-character foundations are not finished production character art or native AR.

The close camera stays behind the selected explorer and enlarges the provisional avatar, using the same walking and action state as the other views. The eye-level view has no floating rear-head overlay, keeping its actual first-person viewpoint clear. After graphics loss, the interactive 2D map explains the state and offers a fresh 3D retry while retaining the selected place, player and route.

Street view now supports drag/arrow-key look and optical pinch/wheel/button zoom while retaining the actual 1.7 metre eye position. Optional device compass begins only after an explicit action and yields to manual look. Follow cameras shorten their boom against loaded building volumes; incomplete map geometry is not world physics or an indoor navigation guarantee.

Optional discovery fog marks unexplored geographic cells, with actual gaps for the session preview and private saved areas. Zoomed-out or horizon-wide views defer fog to bound work. Manual walking and unrecorded device fixes remain ephemeral. With the exploration release flag and verified account service enabled, users can explicitly save coarse live areas each mounted session, delete saved areas and retry interrupted requests. No raw GPS track is stored. The server derives cells, binds observations to the acknowledged recording generation, rejects stale/inaccurate/implausible travel, enforces account limits and never awards visits, XP, offers or quest progress for browser observations. Production account/provider gates remain closed until their separate acceptance requirements pass.

Restaurant encounters add independently approved item pools beneath the selected venue. A mall requires an explicit shop selection; public nearby hints send venue IDs without uploading GPS. Starting an encounter reserves no stock and reveals no outcome. A fresh, single-use staff code lets the server draw and save one non-transferable collectible or linked published offer. Inventory is reachable through Collections and Passport; only current venue staff can confirm offer redemption. Collectibles have no monetary value, and approved offer terms are retained without inventing discounts. The original SVG/CSS chest opens after the granted inventory item is acknowledged. `FOOD_GO_ENCOUNTERS_ENABLED` remains false by default, no real pools or offers were seeded, and deployed customer/staff, contention and physical-device acceptance remain required. See [the encounter delivery](docs/progress/25-restaurant-encounters.md).

The light market-signage design uses Barlow Condensed for destination names, Source Sans 3 for reading, and the existing Bricolage Grotesque wordmark. Deep ink and petrol ground the yellow masthead, mandarin actions and paper surfaces. The five customer destinations are Feed, Quests, Explore, Deals and Outback, in that order. Explore opens first and stays third, at the centre. Passport and its personal wall, FoodDex, VenueDex and food trails remain reachable from the account and collection controls.

Quests are genuine restaurant support requests across exploration, trying a dish, private feedback, community stories and promotions. A verified partner submits a bounded campaign for independent operator review. Completion requires a fresh single-use staff code; dish, promotion and story requirements are verified by the server. Private feedback can be critical and a public positive review is never a condition. Once-only awards and Sydney-day limits protect the progression ledger.

The shared account ladder has 250 levels. The XP curve grows sharply after level 100, where verified visits, cuisine diversity, completed quest categories, published original stories and Outback achievements also gate advancement. Mighty, Golden and Legendary profile treatments use this server-derived status. Signed-in reach continues to count distinct eligible accounts per story; browser movement and cosmetics never earn verified XP.

Outback is an original Australian village, with one permanent state or territory destination and a 16-plot isometric farm. Clearing, construction, upgrades, crop planting and harvests spend server-owned resources and use server time. Daily supplies require genuine recent activity. Its first release has a farmhouse, gardens, sheds, water tanks and bakeries. Combat, paid shortcuts, regional climate simulation, seasonal events, CardDex and CreatureDex are not part of this delivery. The village uses original artwork, not Clash of Clans assets.

The owner clarified that other pages should have one natural document scroll with compact sections. Keep the first screen useful and retain longer content below it; do not freeze or hide an entire Feed, collection or Passport inside a single viewport. Passport's personal wall should sit below identity, in the familiar profile-and-timeline pattern, with posting and existing social actions. Extra game/profile detail expands on request so the wall is not buried beneath every milestone and activity entry.

The community experience has a public wall and personal walls where people share thoughts and food experiences, then like, comment and share posts. Food Go should carry familiar social interactions in its own visual identity. Public content needs moderation and reporting; users need blocking and control over their own posts. Clearly label visibility, moderation state and fictional previews. Personal media upload, following, messaging and notifications are separate features unless explicitly delivered.

Feed content and personal walls require a signed-in account aged 16 or over. Signup collects a private date of birth; existing and phone accounts declare it once in Passport. The backend uses the stored self-declaration and Sydney calendar, not editable metadata or client claims. Unknown age and failed age checks stay closed. Feed keeps its original first navigation slot for everyone; its destination explains the age requirement when content is restricted. Explore remains centred. Verified safety reporting, blocking and deletion remain possible without opening Feed. This is self-declared eligibility, not identity-based age verification.

Community participation requires explicit acceptance of the current acknowledgement. Registration shows the rules, and a verified account records its acceptance before posts, comments, likes or shares. Browsing, safety reporting, blocking and deleting one's own posts remain available without acceptance. Prohibit abuse and misleading or unlawful content while allowing truthful, respectful criticism and lawful complaints. Do not promise automatic legal liability or bans on separate platforms. See [the acknowledgement policy](docs/security/community-acknowledgement.md).
